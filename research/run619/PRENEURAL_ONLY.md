## New analytic identifiability boundary (elementary linear algebra, NOT a novel theorem)

For a three-class linear decoder with hidden width 39, let `D_q` contain rows `W_q-W_j` for `j != q`. The decoder-normal subspace `N_q=row(D_q)` has rank at most 2, and its orthogonal complement `K_q=ker(D_q)` has dimension at least **37**. For LSTM full (h,c) width 78 with a decoder on h alone, the full-state decoder-null dimension is at least **76**. Consequently raw full-state null retention will be dimension-biased; h and c must be reported separately. The present decoder-null condition `D_q delta_h=0` does **not** imply future readout silence: under shared bit b, `D_{q'}[T_b(h+delta_h)-T_b(h)]` can be nonzero, with first-order cross-coupling `D_{q'} J_b(h) P_K delta_h`. This is ordinary linearization/observability, not a theorem of neural neutrality.

Explicit synthetic counterexample (not a learned network or physical reachability witness): logits `(x,0,-10)`, initial states `h+=(1,+0.6)`, `h-=(1,-0.6)` have identical initial class and margin; their difference is entirely in decoder-null y. Shared affine input map `T(x,y)=(0.4x+0.8y,0.2y)` gives x'=.88 and x'=-.08, opposite decoded classes. Therefore any claim that decoder-null displacement is causally harmless requires a **future-invariance** test, not just a present-time projection.

For a physically reachable same-q pair, decompose `delta_h=P_N delta_h+P_K delta_h`. The new falsifiable observable is the next-bit decoder-margin difference `y=D_{q'}(T_b(h_A)-T_b(h_B))`. Test the **incremental** contribution of calibration-only `D_{q'} J_b P_K delta_h` over a normal-only predictor, with calibration-only mean-J and distance/ridge controls. Do not evaluate the heldout Jacobian. This is predictive evidence only; on-manifold input-history substitutions also change lexical structure and cannot isolate a pure neutrality-specific mediation effect.
