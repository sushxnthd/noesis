# Run288 targeted primary-priority collision audit (2026-10-20)

1. Butoi et al., ICLR 2025, "Training Neural Networks as Recognizers of Formal Languages" (https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7256b2c07b6980aca382eb41606501c2-Abstract-Conference.html): direct formal-language recognition benchmarks across simple RNN, LSTM and transformer. NOESIS cannot claim novelty for finite-length generalization gaps or architecture comparisons alone.
2. Nowak, Cotterell & Boumasmoud, ICML 2026, "An Algebraic View of the Expressivity of Recurrent Language Models" (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/nowak26b.html): the arithmetic model changes which formal languages RNNs can implement. NOESIS's rational certificates use exact arithmetic on stored binary64 weights; they do **not** automatically certify IEEE-754 rounding semantics for arbitrarily long inference. Future certificates must explicitly declare semantics and ideally add outward-rounded float verification.
3. Bauer et al., ICML 2026, "A unified theory of feature learning in RNNs and DNNs" (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/bauer26a.html): already predicts learning phase transitions in weight-sharing recurrent systems; a generic claim that training produces a transition is not novel without a distinct mechanism.
4. Pert, Alrajeh & Russo, 2025, "RNN Generalization to Omega-Regular Languages" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02491): finite extrapolation on automata-derived tasks is already empirically studied; exact all-length proofs remain a meaningful distinction, but not a first demonstration of formal-language generalization.

**Priority verdict:** Run288's supported claim is a frozen, reproduced, within-architecture prediction of future exact certificate discoverability. It is not a first generalization transition, a complete correctness diagnostic, or a model-agnostic law. Arithmetic-model ambiguity is a specific unresolved correctness-transfer gap.
