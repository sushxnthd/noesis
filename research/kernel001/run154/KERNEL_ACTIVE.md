1. **Question:** Can an all-alphabet, task-coupled *reachable-region inductive simulation* certify that a gradient-trained recurrent network implements reusable counting rather than a finite-horizon heuristic, without assuming that observed ReLU activation masks remain fixed?
2. **Primary frontier:** El-Naggar et al., ICGI/PMLR 217 (2023) prove exact counting conditions for single-cell ReLU RNNs; piecewise-affine invariant verification is established. The new obligation is a trained, multidimensional, task-coupled certificate with adversarial controls.
3. **Exact success:** At least one independently machine-checked **all-length** task-simulation invariant for an unchanged, gradient-trained multidimensional network, while rejecting the finite-cap spoof and the Run154 seed225 delayed-mask failure.
4. **Strongest alternative:** The certificate encodes the target counter in its abstraction or requires architecture-specific weight tying; a bounded heuristic can spoof its finite queries.
5. **Cheapest falsification:** Apply the checker first to exact cap spoofs and seed225; any false acceptance immediately kills that proposed sufficient condition.
6. **Publishable threshold:** Primary priority audit, independent checker, strongest spoof controls, prospective prediction on unseen width/task family, smallest supported claim, and demonstrated learning transition.
7. **Learning relevance:** Apply without changing weights to ordinary gradient-trained checkpoints, and identify a measurable training or generalization transition beyond training loss.
8. **Kill:** After three substantive unsuccessful strengthening attempts, retire or reformulate this kernel; do not rename finite-horizon accuracy a certificate.
