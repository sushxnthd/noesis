## Exact classical theorem (separately frozen)
**Theorem protocol SHA256:** `365b1702d11fdd461cf8e0d6fa9c131db26c98e8edadf94ec97dbdd8ed4bc734`.

For any deterministic K-class automaton, K>=2, and fixed block length L>=3, define a time-homogeneous clock lift with state (q,p), transition (q,p) --a--> (delta(q,a),(p+1) mod L), and initial phase p=0 at block start. The good readout is g(q) at every phase. The bad readout is g(q) at phase p=1, but (g(q)+1) mod K at all other phases. After **any number** of complete length-L blocks followed by a suffix-1 probe, phase is 1 and both readouts agree with the exact oracle. After any proper prefix of a block, t=1..L-1, and the same suffix-1 probe, phase is (t+1) mod L !=1 and the bad readout is wrong. Therefore even **infinite correct complete-block boundary observations cannot imply intermediate readout correctness**. This is classical finite-state observational nonidentifiability, not a theorem about an actual learned RNN. It assumes aligned fixed-length blocks and the stated suffix-1 query protocol.

Independent exact enumeration: **36,864** fixed-boundary candidate strings, **47** admissible exact lexical-class members, **12,705** independent full-string/streaming oracle checks, **279** switched neutral histories depth0..4, **1,116/1,116** correct boundary observations and **10,602/10,602** deliberately incorrect interior observations, no counterexamples. The initial oracle-checker failure and corrected rerun are preserved. The theorem does **not** contradict R418/R422's conditional all-future **boundary-only** certificates.
